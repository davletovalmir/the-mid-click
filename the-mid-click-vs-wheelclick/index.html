The Mid Click vs WheelClick

WheelClick is the successor to the open-source MiddleClick, rebuilt from scratch by the same developer and released in 2026. It does the same core thing as The Mid Click, a three-finger click or tap on the trackpad becomes a middle click, and then adds a lot more. Here's an honest comparison, with the facts taken from WheelClick's own site and README.

Where WheelClick is the better pick

If you want more than a middle click, WheelClick wins on breadth: Magic Mouse support, a force-click trigger, per-app rules, autoscroll, and an App Store listing with Apple handling updates. Its free tier also covers the exact thing The Mid Click charges for, a three-finger click or tap on the trackpad, so if price is the only question, WheelClick is the cheaper answer. It also reaches back to macOS 12, one release further than The Mid Click.

Where The Mid Click is the better pick

  • Four fingers. If a three-finger tap collides with a gesture you already use, The Mid Click moves to four. WheelClick lists only three-finger gestures.
  • Seeing what's happening. The test pad shows which mouse button arrived and the dots show how many fingers the trackpad reports, which is how you find out in ten seconds whether a gesture conflict or a permission is the problem. That matters most on the day you grant Accessibility.
  • Nothing phoning home. The Mid Click sends no usage data at all; WheelClick sends anonymous gesture counts daily unless you switch it off. Both are reasonable choices, but if you'd rather not have a switch to find, this is the simpler one. The full privacy policy is one screen.
  • Two settings. Fingers and trigger, and that's the whole app. If you want a tool that never grows, this is it.

How they read the trackpad

MiddleClick, the predecessor, depended on Apple's private MultitouchSupport framework, which is what started crashing on macOS 27. WheelClick's site says the app contains no private frameworks, and The Mid Click reads touches through the gesture events macOS documents. So on that point the two modern apps are alike, and either should survive OS updates better than the original. Here's how the touch stream becomes button 3.

Which should you use?

  • WheelClick if you own a Magic Mouse, want force click or fn+Click, want per-app rules, or simply want three-finger middle click for free.
  • The Mid Click if you need four fingers, want to see the trackpad's finger count and the received button on screen, prefer an app with no telemetry, or like a tool with two settings and nothing else.

Don't run both at once; you'll get two middle clicks per tap.
